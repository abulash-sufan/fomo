India

Supreme Court declares internet access a fundamental right for children

  • What happened: The Supreme Court declared access to and use of the internet a fundamental right for every individual, including children, under Articles 21 and 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.
  • Key details: The bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and K Vinod Chandran stated that safety and freedom for children are not at odds, and blanket bans on internet access would be absurd and counterproductive.
  • Reactions: The court observed that for many children, especially in remote areas or with special needs, the internet is often the only source of learning, information, and companionship.
  • What's next: The court directed strengthening mechanisms to detect and remove child sexual exploitation material online, rather than imposing blanket restrictions on children's access.

  • Autism stem cell therapy

    The Supreme Court directed the Centre to launch a dedicated online portal for autism patients undergoing stem cell therapy within six weeks. The order, passed on October 13, requires patients, guardians, and clinics to maintain detailed records including medical intervention details and practitioner information on the portal. The bench emphasized confidentiality and security of patient data, building on its January 30 order restricting stem cell therapy to approved clinical trials only. The Centre must also issue a protocol explaining rules for clinical trials and research, with a proposed multicentre RCT to be set up in two tertiary care sites within six months.

    Sources (1)Hindustan Times
  • FSSAI sugar ban

    India's food safety regulator proposed a ban on adding sucrose and fructose to infant nutrition products for babies up to six months of age. The draft amendment, published in the Gazette on October 16, aims to limit early exposure to added sugars during a critical growth period and reduce childhood obesity risks. The move follows a 2024 controversy where Nestlé was found adding sugar to Cerelac in India while offering sugar-free versions in Europe. Existing rules allow lactose and glucose polymers as preferred carbohydrates; the proposal removes sucrose and fructose for infants under six months, with the baby food market valued at $1.16 billion in 2024.

Veteran Tamil music composer Deva dies at 75 in Chennai

  • What happened: Deva, veteran music composer, died in Chennai on October 17, 2026, at age 75 after complaining of chest pain and being taken to Surya Hospital.
  • Key details: Known as Thenisai Thendral, Deva composed music for over 400 films across Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and Kannada cinema, and introduced folk-inspired Gaana music to mainstream Tamil cinema.
  • Reactions: Tamil Nadu CM Vijay, film personalities, Vice President C.P. Radhakrishnan, Thirumavalavan, and others paid tributes, recalling his contributions and kind-hearted nature.
  • What's next: Deva's funeral will be held in his native village Mangadu near Arcot on Sunday, October 18, 2026, with state honours; his body will be kept at his T. Nagar residence until 1 p.m. for last respects.
