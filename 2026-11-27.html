Tamil Nadu

Supreme Court finds Tamil Nadu wilfully disobeyed order, orders lamp lit November 28

  • The ruling: On Friday the Supreme Court held the Tamil Nadu government and Madurai administration had prima facie "wilfully and flagrantly disobeyed" its November 20 order, calling the Collector's status report one "shedding crocodile tears."
  • What's next: The Bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and VM Pancholi directed that the lamp be lit at the Deepathoon on Saturday, November 28, four days after the November 24 festival, with all devotees allowed.
  • Contempt notices: Show-cause contempt notices went to the Chief Secretary, DGP, HR&CE Commissioner, the temple Board of Trustees, its chairperson and trustees, and Madurai collector Nishant Krishna.
  • Temple taken over: The Court suspended the Board of Trustees, appointed former Madras High Court judges S Vaidyanathan and V Parthiban as receivers, and directed CRPF to secure the temple and protect the dargah.
  • The flame: Receivers must ensure the lamp is lit using flame from the lamp at the Uchipillaiyar temple; devotees may light it at least 50 metres from the dargah.
  • Governor's move: Governor Rajendra Vishwanath Arlekar sought a report from CM C. Joseph Vijay under Article 167(b) on compliance, after the lamp was lit only at the Deepa Mandapam on November 24.
  • Collector's report: The Madurai Collector told the court he had made every arrangement, but the Arulmigu Subramaniyaswamy Temple Board of Trustees failed to authorise a lamp-lighting team.
