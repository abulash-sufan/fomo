Bollywood actor Tiger Shroff survives Mumbai shooting attempt

  • What happened: Bollywood actor Tiger Shroff survived a shooting attempt on Sunday evening while leaving a football ground in Juhu, Mumbai. At least two men fired multiple shots at close range at his moving car.
  • Key details: Shroff’s driver and an autorickshaw driver were injured. Police recovered the bike used by assailants near Andheri Railway Station and detained two persons for questioning. Three suspects were detained in Rajasthan, including a minor.
  • Reactions: Maharashtra CM Devendra Fadnavis called the incident serious and condemnable, saying suspects would be arrested soon. Deputy CM Eknath Shinde visited Shroff and said such intimidation would not be tolerated. The All Indian Cine Workers Association condemned the attack, alleging gangsters target film industry to extort money.
  • What's next: Rajasthan police said two suspects are from Haryana and Bihar and had lived in Mumbai for years; they will be handed over to Mumbai Police. Authorities are scanning CCTV footage and using facial recognition to trace assailants who fled by train. Police have not confirmed gang involvement despite social media claims linking Lawrence Bishnoi gang.

Three killed in Manipur ambush on firewood collectors

  • What happened: Three people were killed and three others injured after suspected militants ambushed firewood collectors along the Imphal East-Kangpokpi district border in Manipur on Monday morning. The attack took place around 7am in Khongbal Sunurok Uyok.

  • What's next: Markets and shops across Imphal shut following the incident. Protests spread, with road blockades at Khurai Lamlong and a women's demonstration at Ema Keithel market. Security was tightened around the CM's bungalow and leading routes.

World

India issues safety advisory for nationals in Saudi Arabia

  • What happened: India issued a fresh safety advisory for its nationals in Saudi Arabia on Sunday following Houthi drone and missile strikes, after an October 10 attack at Riyadh airport killed an Indian national and injured several others.
  • Key details: The embassy urged Indians to seek shelter inside buildings or interior rooms away from windows during an aerial attack and to refrain from filming or sharing information. Riyadh's international airport remained closed, while airports in Dammam, Jeddah and Medina operated with disruptions.
  • Flight suspensions: Air India, Air India Express and IndiGo suspended flights to and from Saudi Arabia until October 24, while Akasa Air extended suspension until October 27. Saudia, Flynas and Flyadeal flights from Dammam, Jeddah and Medina to India remained available.
  • Casualties: Four injured Indians remained in intensive care, four others were in regular wards, and three had been discharged. Mohammed Yousef Ali, the Indian national killed in the Riyadh attack, was laid to rest in Saudi Arabia on October 12.
  • Reactions: India's Ambassador to Saudi Arabia and embassy officials attended the funeral and offered condolences. The embassy continued visiting hospitals and coordinating with families of the injured.
  • What's next: The embassy advised Indians planning to travel to Saudi Arabia, including for Umrah, to factor in airport disruptions and possible cancellations. Nationals needing urgent departure may consider transiting via Bahrain, Kuwait or Qatar with necessary visas.

Heavy rain batters Chennai as monsoon intensifies

  • What happened: Chennai was battered by extremely heavy rainfall on Saturday night as the Northeast monsoon intensified, with many areas receiving over 20 cm of rain, leading to flooding of localities and arterial roads.
  • Key details: Parrys recorded the maximum rainfall of 36 cm, followed by Kathivakkam with 34.4 cm; six places recorded extremely heavy rainfall and 17 places recorded very heavy rainfall (11.5 cm–20.4 cm).
  • Reactions: Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay visited the Integrated Command and Control Centre on Sunday afternoon, reviewed preparedness, and appreciated officials for their tireless work over two days, urging coordinated departmental efforts.
  • What's next: Heavy to very heavy rainfall is likely across Tamil Nadu till October 24, with the IMD attributing the rain to an upper air cyclonic circulation over north Tamil Nadu and adjoining south coastal Andhra Pradesh.
