Nvidia probes CEO Huang over alleged China spy links; shares tumble

  • What happened: Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation on Sunday into whether CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation following media reports of his relationship with a Chinese businesswoman.
  • Key details: The New York Times reported US intelligence concluded Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, was a Chinese intelligence asset who gathered information on Huang and potentially influenced chip sales to China.
  • CEO response: Huang admitted to infidelity in a staff memo but previously told Bloomberg his interactions with Alice Huang were 'completely normal and not unusual' business dealings with Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner.
  • Market impact: Nvidia shares fell over 4% in overnight trading and more than 6% in Frankfurt, wiping out roughly $270 billion in market value, while key Asian suppliers TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix rebounded.
  • Reactions: China's Foreign Ministry called the spy narrative 'groundless accusation, slander, and a smear'; investor Bill Ackman said if it is more than an affair it is a 'real problem' and Elon Musk agreed.
  • What's next: The board stated Huang fully backs the review and prioritises US national and economic security; his daughter Madison Huang cancelled a planned trip to Seoul for an Nvidia conference.

World

Houthis hit Aramco Tower in Riyadh as Saudi intercepts missiles

  • What happened: Houthi rebels launched strikes on Riyadh on Saturday, hitting the Aramco Tower in the capital's central financial district. The group also targeted Riyadh's airport and Aramco oil storage facilities in separate strikes.
  • Key details: Saudi energy ministry said the tower suffered only minor damage with no deaths or injuries. BBC Verify footage showed more extensive damage, with at least one floor badly hit and windows shattered on more than one level. Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed several missiles.
  • Reactions: The Houthis confirmed targeting Riyadh's business zone via Saba news agency but did not name the Aramco Tower. Saudi Arabia has not commented on the alleged strike in Yemen that Houthis say killed at least 33 people, including women and children, while Saba said 39 were injured.
  • What's next: Major fighting has resumed between Houthi forces and Yemen's Saudi-backed government troops after an informal ceasefire unravelled in July. The UN says more than 1,600 people have been killed and more than 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed.
  • Background: A Saudi-led coalition has fought the Houthis for over a decade in support of Yemen's internationally recognised government. Fighting intensified after the Iran war broke out, and Houthis announced a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia in July.
  • Numbers: More than 1,600 killed and more than 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed, according to the United Nations.
  • Outlets disagree: NYT says falling shrapnel hit the building; France 24 says falling debris damaged it. BBC says footage shows missile or drone strike damage.
