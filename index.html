India-US trade pact near finish line, may be sealed soon: Jaishankar

  • What happened: External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar said India and the US have sorted out the bulk of issues on the proposed trade deal, which could be sealed "soon" as talks enter the last stretch.
  • Key details: He said many remaining concerns are "not so much at our end" because changes have been more on the American side, and refused to set a timeframe, urging people to "bear with it".
  • Key details: Talks began in early 2025 after the Trump administration imposed 50% tariffs on Indian exports; they gained pace after an understanding this year to cut tariffs to 18%, though the judiciary later scrapped Trump's tariffs.
  • Reaction: Jaishankar said the deal must be "beneficial, balanced" and give India a comparative advantage over comparable economies, noting the US is India's biggest market. He said the Democratic mid-term win would "change many things" but not on defence, security or tech.
  • What's next: The pact may be announced around PM Modi's likely US visit for the G20 Summit in Miami on December 14-15, where he and President Trump may review ties. Rubio said last month in Mumbai both sides are very close.

Rahul Gandhi launches Telangana gig workers welfare scheme in Hyderabad

  • What happened: Congress leader Rahul Gandhi joined a bike rally with gig workers in Hyderabad on Saturday and launched the Telangana Gig Workers' Social Security and Welfare Scheme, riding pillion on a scooty driven by a female worker.
  • Key details: Telangana CM A Revanth Reddy and other Congress leaders joined the rally from Shilparamam to Jayabheri Ground. The scheme falls under the platform-based gig workers act passed in March this year.
  • Key details: The act requires valid written reason and seven days' notice for termination, timely payments and safe work conditions. Workers get Rs five lakh accident and Rs 10 lakh health insurance plus wi-fi enabled rest zones.
  • Reaction: He attacked the BJP-RSS, saying its ideology benefits big capitalists like "Adani, Ambani" while treating workers like bonded labourers.

Microsoft restores Outlook, Teams and Copilot after 36-hour global outage

  • The outage: Microsoft tools including Outlook, Teams and Copilot failed from around noon Eastern time on Thursday, spreading to more than 70 countries and affecting millions of users, per Downdetector.
  • Cause: Microsoft said the problem came from a "network capacity upgrade" and was a technical issue, not a cyberattack or an AI agent. It apologised for the disruption.
  • Restoration: Service was gradually restored through Friday, and Microsoft said early Saturday it had fully recovered Teams, Outlook, Copilot and other apps. NYT put the disruption at about 36 hours; other outlets said over 24 hours.
  • Who was hit: The Japanese government, the Philippines Supreme Court, the Australian spy agency, UK NHS parts, Canadian rail and hospitals, Singapore systems and the US House were affected. In India, work was disrupted on Friday.
  • FAA link: Microsoft said it had no indication that a Federal Aviation Administration ground stop issued late Friday was connected to the outage.
  • Markets: Microsoft shares closed nearly flat on Friday despite the outage, according to Livemint.

Stalin seeks all-party meet on Mekedatu dam; Congress splits

  • Stalin's letter: DMK president M.K. Stalin wrote to Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay on November 14, 2026, urging an all-party meeting after Karnataka CM D.K.
  • Key demands: Stalin sought a delegation of Tamil Nadu MPs to meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi and submit resolutions from the proposed all-party meeting and the Assembly's June resolution opposing the dam.
  • Unity call: "We should bury our differences and remain united on the Cauvery water dispute," Stalin said, adding that Tamil Nadu could not stay silent when Karnataka's Deputy CM was vocal about the dam plan.
  • Legal route: Stalin said past experience showed negotiations with Karnataka would not secure Tamil Nadu's rights and legal remedies must be pursued, cautioning against Vijay's visit to Karnataka to meet the CM.
  • Congress split: TNCC chief Manickam Tagore opposed Shivakumar's appeal to PM Modi, calling the dam illegal and harmful to delta farmers, alleging it violated Supreme Court directives and the Cauvery tribunal's final award.
  • Shivakumar's reply: Shivakumar dismissed Tagore's objection, saying he was "least bothered" and "It is my state, my right," citing the Supreme Court's 170 TMC water allocation and offering to explain the project to a TN delegation.
