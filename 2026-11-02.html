Supreme Court dismisses Centre's challenge to Vedanta arbitration awards

  • What happened: The Supreme Court on Monday dismissed the Union government's challenge to a Delhi High Court order allowing mining major Vedanta Ltd. and Ravva Oil (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. to enforce foreign arbitration awards involving claims of nearly $99 million.
  • Reaction: Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Manmohan questioned why the government continued to oppose enforcement, warning such delays could undermine India's standing as an arbitration hub: 'What are these arguments? You are frustrating an arbitral award like this?...
  • Background: The dispute dates back to a 1994 production-sharing contract for the Ravva oilfield in the Krishna-Godavari basin, signed between the Union government and a consortium comprising ONGC, Videocon Petroleum, Ravva Oil and Cairn Energy. Cairn Energy's stake later passed to Cairn India and then Vedanta.
  • Timeline: In October 2004, an arbitral tribunal seated in Kuala Lumpur delivered a partial award; the final award followed in October 2016. The Centre challenged both, but the Malaysian Federal Court upheld them in 2011 and February 2019. In July 2014, the government sought recovery of about $64 million from Vedanta and $35 million from Ravva Oil.

World

Ukraine and Russia emergency apps hacked with fake nuclear alerts

  • What happened: On Sunday evening, Ukraine's Air Alert app sent fake warnings of nuclear radiation, chemical threats, street combat, and Oreshnik missile deployment to users in and around Kyiv, causing public alarm before authorities clarified it was a hack within an hour.
  • Ukraine reaction: Ukraine's Air Force blamed 'enemy influence' and the Center for Strategic Communications pointed to Russian language in messages as evidence of Russian responsibility, while confirming the capital's separate alert app functioned normally.
  • Russia incident: Hours later on Monday, Russia's Emergency Ministry reported a 'massive cyberattack' on its alert app that sent fake warnings of drone attacks and nuclear warheads launched by Ukrainian drones, instructing Russians to stockpile food and hide.
  • Hacker claim: Russian Telegram channel Mash reported the hacking group Hitcore claimed responsibility for the Ukrainian app breach, though this could not be independently verified.
  • Operational impact: Ukraine's air force spokesperson Yurii Ihnat stated the breach did not affect military command systems or real-time threat communications, which continued via independent channels.
  • Kremlin response: Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov acknowledged constant cyber threats against Russian agencies, including increasing AI-driven attacks, and urged all government bodies to improve digital cybersecurity.
  • Ceasefire context: The attacks coincided with the expiry of a six-day US-brokered humanitarian ceasefire in Russian-occupied Kherson region, which had allowed aid and evacuations in Olekshy and was described as the longest pause in the four-year war.

  • Cyclone status: Cyclone Murasu intensified into a Severe Cyclonic Storm over West Central and Southwest Bay of Bengal on November 2, with wind speeds of 100-110 km/h gusting to 120 km/h, set to make landfall on November 3 between Nellore and Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh.
  • Rainfall alert: Heavy to very heavy rain expected across Tamil Nadu over next three days; November 1 isolated heavy rain in Tiruvallur, November 2 heavy rain in Chennai and Tiruvallur, with continued rain in Chengalpattu, Kancheepuram, Ranipet, Vellore and Nilgiris.
